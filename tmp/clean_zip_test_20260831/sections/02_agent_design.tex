\section{Submitted policy and safety layers}

\paragraph{State and memory.}
The dispatcher locks a family--role branch for each game. The policy is
deterministic conditional on the complete visible history, persistent memory,
and game identifier---not on the current state alone. Bargaining memory stores
opponent demands and rejected shares by role, information regime, discount
configuration, and disclosed opponent; negotiation stores role-specific price
paths; the persuasion buyer updates signal precision only when purchase reveals
quality. Undisclosed opponents receive game-local keys. Messages verbalize an
already selected action, although they may indirectly affect later states.

\paragraph{Bargaining.}
For pot $M$, both roles predict the opponent's demanded share from a robust
median step, blend it with a configuration prior, and search responder shares
$y\in\{0.10,0.105,\ldots,0.94\}$. The score
\begin{equation}
 \sigma((y-f+0.007)/w)(1-y)^\gamma-
 [1-\sigma((y-f+0.007)/w)]c
 \label{eq:bargaining-search}
\end{equation}
uses role-specific floor $f$, width $w$, curvature $\gamma$, and delay cost
$c$ listed in Appendix~\ref{app:policy}. Alice uses the resulting branch
directly. Bob then transfers
\begin{equation}
 T=\min\{M[r(\delta_B)+0.008t],\max(0,x_B-0.24M)\},
 \quad r=0.018\;\text{if }\delta_B<0.95\text{ else }0.008,
 \label{eq:bob-transfer}
\end{equation}
and may override a rejection when the received share exceeds
$\max\{0.16,(0.30\text{ if }\delta_B<0.95\text{ else }0.33)-0.09t-0.055s\}$.
These are hand-set heuristics, not Rubinstein equilibrium claims.

\paragraph{Negotiation.}
Complete-information targets allocate a declining fraction of feasible surplus;
hidden-information targets extrapolate half the opponent's median favorable
price step. Offers remain on the individually rational side of own value.
Responses compare offered utility with a declining continuation threshold;
repeated profitable offers can be accepted and repeated infeasible unknown-
horizon paths can trigger walkaway. Buyer and seller constants differ, and the
seller has a short-horizon midpoint repair; Appendix~\ref{app:policy} specifies
every branch.

\paragraph{Persuasion.}
The buyer purchases below low value, passes above high value, and otherwise
compares price with a conservative posterior expected value. The seller always
recommends high quality. With known buyer values $v>u$, price $P$, prior $p$,
and a \emph{static randomized} model $\Pr(+\mid H)=1$,
$\Pr(+\mid L)=q$, purchase after ``+'' requires
\begin{equation}
 c=(P-u)/(v-u),\qquad
 q\le q_{\max}=\min\{1,p(1-c)/[c(1-p)]\}.
 \label{eq:static-calibration}
\end{equation}
V33 sets a target $q=0.88q_{\max}$ off-terminal and $0.94q_{\max}$ terminal
(capped at $0.92$), then deterministically signals the $n$th observed low item
when $\lfloor(n+1)q\rfloor>\lfloor nq\rfloor$. Because $q$ changes and the
schedule can be inferred, Eq.~\ref{eq:static-calibration} is only a calibration
heuristic: it does not prove history-conditional obedience. When receiver values
are hidden, $q=\operatorname{clip}(0.12+0.28p+0.05\mathbf{1}_{\rm terminal},0,0.40)$
is explicitly ad hoc and carries no obedience interpretation.

\paragraph{Validation and exposure.}
Actions are checked for exact keys, finite values, budget balance, legal enums,
required counteroffers, and message length; exceptions invoke a legal fallback.
The controller queues one assignment, leaves on assignment, drains the game,
and audits authoritative counts and ratings. Predeclared family and role
loss/drawdown thresholds bound exposure but are not statistical tests.
