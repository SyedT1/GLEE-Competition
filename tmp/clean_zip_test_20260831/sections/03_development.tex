\section{Failure-driven development}

Three observed failures became executable invariants. First, an early legal
policy cycled for 99 rounds under an unknown horizon; repeated-state counters
now activate bounded matching, acceptance, or walkaway. Second, a blocking
concurrency-four request for 24 games returned after 53 and lost 44.84 rating
points; the submitted controller therefore admits one assignment and audits the
authoritative count after every game. Third, aggregate gains repeatedly hid a
losing role, motivating fixed role branches and role-local guards. Appendix~
\ref{app:policy} maps the historical version labels to deployed rules.

These rules were selected after inspecting earlier live batches. The final
trace is consequently post-selection evidence, not a held-out test. Thresholds
were operational risk choices rather than values derived from a loss function
or false-stop guarantee.
