\section{Supplementary statistical audit}
\label{app:statistics}

\paragraph{Sources, linkage, and coverage.}
The rating analysis uses all 248 live rows in
\path{glee_v33_rating_outcomes.csv} after excluding the single synthetic
contract-test row. The text export contains 220 complete game records: 127 of
150 bargaining assignments, 74 of 78 persuasion assignments, and 19 of 20
negotiation assignments (88.7\% overall coverage). Two records omit their
repeated family/transcript header but retain a separate \emph{Game setup} and
\emph{Final result}; the parser recovers them as distinct games. The export has
no game IDs and is missing 28 rated assignments, so transcript records are not
positionally joined to rating rows. Transcript-derived quantities below are
available-case behavioral summaries rather than rating-conditioned outcomes.

\paragraph{Rating distributions and sequential behavior.}
Table~\ref{tab:v33-distribution} reports robust and conventional descriptive
statistics. The positive bargaining mean coexists with an almost even sign
split; persuasion has a positive mean but a negative median and 40 of 78
changes are negative. The ordinary IID intervals and sign tests are descriptive
diagnostics only: they do not correct for repeated opponents, context drift,
family ordering, adaptive policy development, or optional stopping. Lag-one
autocorrelations were $0.05$, $-0.08$, and $0.07$ for bargaining, persuasion,
and negotiation respectively; the corresponding maximum losing streaks were
five, four, and six assignments. These small autocorrelations do not establish
independence.

Figure~\ref{fig:v33-sequential} plots cumulative changes against the actual
family baseline-loss and moving peak-drawdown boundaries. Bargaining's maximum
within-run drawdown was 24.75 points but did not trigger the 24-point guard
because that early drawdown occurred before the 20-game activation point.
Persuasion finished at its maximum drawdown of 23.19 points and crossed its
22-point boundary at game 78. Negotiation never exceeded its initial rating and
crossed the 30-point drawdown boundary at game 20.

\paragraph{Transcript outcomes.}
Among the 127 exported bargaining games, 113 reached agreement (89.0\%) and 14
ended in walkaway. Agreement rates were 47/54 for Alice and 66/73 for Bob.
Agreements used a median of one observed round (mean 1.40, maximum 12).
Negotiation produced 11/19 agreements, four no-deals, and four walkaways; role
splits were 6/11 agreements for buyers and 5/8 for sellers. Negotiation
agreements used a median of four observed rounds, compared with one for
no-deals and 3.5 for walkaways. Missing transcripts prevent treating these
rates as full-run estimates.

\paragraph{Persuasion behavior.}
The 74 exported persuasion games contain 1,480 round decisions. In the 40 games
where V33 was seller, all 397 observed high-quality products received a positive
signal. Of 403 low-quality products, 135 received a positive signal and 268 a
negative one, a 33.5\% low-quality pooling frequency; hence 397/532 positive
signals were high quality (74.6\% empirical precision). Opposing buyers bought
309/532 positive-signal products and 24/268 negative-signal products. In the 34
games where V33 was buyer, it purchased 350/680 products; 245 purchases were
high quality and 105 low quality. Passed-product quality is hidden in this role,
so buyer precision is evaluated only on the selectively revealed purchased
subset. A lexical classifier matching the submitted policy's signal-polarity
rules classified all V33 seller messages; it left 206 opponent messages
unclassified, and no claims use those messages as positive or negative.

\paragraph{Context and stopping sensitivity.}
Table~\ref{tab:v33-context-distribution} aggregates the recorded policy context
keys; the machine-readable CSV retains the complete role, information, horizon,
discount, signal-mode, prior, and receiver-cutoff buckets. Table~\ref{tab:v33-stop-sensitivity}
replays the observed rating sequence while jointly scaling all family and role
loss/drawdown thresholds. At half thresholds, bargaining would have stopped at
game 17, persuasion at 24, and negotiation at 19. At 1.25 times the thresholds,
neither adaptively stopped family would have crossed a guard within the observed
prefix. This counterfactual is path-dependent: it assumes the same later
assignments would have occurred despite a different stopping policy.
