\section{Technical and strategic approach}

\paragraph{Architecture and memory.}
A dispatcher selects a family policy and a role-locked branch for the complete
game. The agent reads only server-filtered state. Memory is keyed by family,
role, information regime, horizon bucket, and opponent when disclosed; updates
use visible offers or purchased-product revelations only. Messages communicate
an already selected action and never determine numeric choices. Consequently,
V33 requires Python and \texttt{glee-sdk}, but no model weights, training,
accelerator, or paid inference API.

\paragraph{Bargaining.}
Every proposal divides pot $M$ exactly, $x_A+x_B=M$. Alice retains the V27
surplus-protecting, cycle-safe branch. Bob uses a delay-aware repair motivated by
his V27 loss and alternating-offers incentives \citep{rubinstein1982perfect}.
With normalized deadline progress $t$, own discount $\delta_B$,
and stall count $s$, Bob transfers from his inherited offer
\begin{equation}
 T=\min\{M[r(\delta_B)+0.008t],\max(0,x_B-0.24M)\},
 \quad
 r(\delta_B)=\begin{cases}0.018&\delta_B<0.95,\\0.008&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
 \label{eq:bob-transfer}
\end{equation}
Only when the inherited V27 branch rejects, Bob's recovery override instead
accepts a received share above
\begin{equation}
 f_B=\max\{0.16,r_f(\delta_B)-0.09t-0.055s\},\quad
 r_f(\delta_B)=\begin{cases}0.30&\delta_B<0.95,\\0.33&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
 \label{eq:bob-floor}
\end{equation}
The transfer cannot push an inherited offer from above to below 24\% of the pot;
$f_B$ is a sufficient recovery threshold, not Bob's global acceptance floor.
Both rules concede more when delay is expensive or the path stalls. Repeated
states activate explicit cycle handling rather than indefinite rejection.

\paragraph{Negotiation.}
For seller value $v_s$, buyer value $v_b$, and price $P$, utilities are
$U_s=P-v_s$ and $U_b=v_b-P$ \citep{nash1950bargaining}. Under complete
information, V33 targets a
role-specific fraction of feasible surplus $S=v_b-v_s$ and reduces that claim
with $t$, observed opponent concessions, and stalls. Under hidden information,
it projects the opponent's robust median price step and keeps counteroffers on
the individually rational side of its own value. A profitable repeated offer
may be accepted; an infeasible repeated path under an unknown horizon triggers
a walkaway. The buyer branch is the exact V23 rollback and the seller is the
V27 branch. This historical selection did not guarantee a positive V33 sample,
which is central to our analysis.

\paragraph{Persuasion.}
The buyer estimates positive-message precision only from quality revealed after
purchase and avoids treating passed products as labeled observations. The
seller always recommends high quality. For known buyer values $v>u$, price $P$,
high-quality prior $p$, and low-quality positive-signal probability $q$, the
purchase cutoff is $c=(P-u)/(v-u)$. For $0<p<1$, $0<c<1$, and a static
signal model with $\Pr(+\mid H)=1$ and $\Pr(+\mid L)=q$, Bayesian obedience
following a positive signal \citep{kamenica2011bayesian} requires
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(H\mid +)=\frac{p}{p+(1-p)q}\ge c,
 \quad q\le q_{\max}=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{p(1-c)}{c(1-p)}\right\}.
 \label{eq:obedience}
\end{equation}
V33 uses the marginal-frequency target $q=0.88q_{\max}$ off-terminal and
$q=\min(0.94q_{\max},0.92)$ terminal. For $c\leq0$ it returns one, for
$c\geq1$ it returns zero, and for interior $c$ with $p\in\{0,1\}$ the
implementation returns zero. Hidden-value play uses
$q=\operatorname{clip}(0.12+0.28p+0.05\mathbf{1}_{\rm terminal},0,0.40)$.
For a fixed target $q$, the $n$th observed low-quality product is recommended
exactly when
\begin{equation}
 a_n=\lfloor(n+1)q\rfloor-\lfloor nq\rfloor=1,
 \label{eq:pacing}
\end{equation}
so a constant-$q$ sequence has prefix discrepancy below one. Because V33
recomputes $q$ and relaxes it terminally, this fixed-target guarantee and the
static posterior bound are design benchmarks, not a claim of round-by-round
obedience against a buyer who can infer the pacing phase.

\paragraph{Action and exposure safety.}
Local validation enforces required keys, finite numeric fields, exact budget
balance, enumerated decisions, counteroffer requirements, and a 2,000-character
message limit. Exceptions activate a legal family fallback and are logged. The
live controller queues one family, leaves immediately after observing an
assignment, drains that game, and then reads authoritative counts and ratings.
For bargaining, negotiation, and persuasion respectively, family baseline loss
limits are $(30,40,28)$ and peak drawdown limits are $(24,30,22)$; these activate
after 20 games. Role baseline limits are $(24,28,20)$ and role drawdowns are
$(18,22,16)$ after ten games in that role. Assignment overshoot, a live fallback,
or a checkpoint-visible telemetry error aborts exposure.
