\section{Agent behavior audit}
\label{sec:behavior}

The executable targets three process properties: individual rationality,
escape from repeated paths, and role-separated evidence. Validators test action
legality; cycle counters test termination behavior; and family/role guards test
exposure. None is a certificate of rating performance. Likewise, the seller's
deterministic frequency target controls aggregate signaling frequency but not
dynamic receiver obedience.

The transcript export covers 220 of 248 rated assignments and omits game IDs,
so behavior cannot be linked safely to assignment rating. In the 127 available
bargaining games, 113 reached agreement (47/54 for Alice and 66/73 for Bob).
This measures agreement behavior, not the effect of the recovery override.

In 40 available seller games, V33 positively signaled all 397 high-quality and
135/403 low-quality products. Of 532 positive signals, 397 were high quality
(74.6\% available-case precision); opposing buyers purchased 309/532 positive-
signal and 24/268 negative-signal products. These aggregate responses neither
establish counterfactual purchase lift nor verify obedience. In 34 buyer games,
V33 purchased 350/680 products (245 high, 105 low); passed quality is hidden, so
this purchased subset is selectively revealed. Negotiation reached 11/19 deals,
with four no-deals and four walkaways, while both role totals were negative.

The audit's defensible conclusion is diagnostic: action validity, agreement,
signal calibration, and rating success are distinct outcomes. The missing ID
linkage prevents attributing rating changes to particular behaviors.
