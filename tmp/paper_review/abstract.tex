\begin{abstract}
We present \AgentName, a deterministic, role-aware agent for GLEE bargaining,
bilateral negotiation, and persuasion. The submitted V33 policy uses no
language model at inference time. It combines incentive-based targets,
opponent-path summaries, a static Bayesian-persuasion benchmark, strict action
validation, and a controller that admits and audits one assignment at a time.
Development exposed three useful failures: a legal policy can cycle for 99
rounds, a blocking SDK batch can overshoot its requested exposure, and positive
family ratings can hide a losing role. V33 therefore locks policies by role and
uses role- and family-level loss guards. In one sequential live run, bargaining
gained 190.94 displayed rating points over 150 games and persuasion gained
55.67 over 78; negotiation lost 33.73 over 20 and stopped on drawdown. Both
bargaining and persuasion roles were positive, while both negotiation roles
were negative. These adaptively stopped observations are descriptive, not
causal comparisons, but demonstrate why strategic policy, behavioral auditing,
and exposure control must be designed together.
\end{abstract}
