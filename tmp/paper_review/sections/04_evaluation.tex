The V33 run began on August 30, 2026 and targeted 150 authoritative completions
per family in the order bargaining, persuasion, negotiation. Matchmaking used
concurrency one, and every completed assignment was credited from the difference
between its immediately adjacent dashboard snapshots. Bargaining reached the
target. Persuasion stopped 23.19 points below its 1849.03 session peak;
negotiation stopped 33.73 below its initial and peak rating. Across the 248 live
rows, role sums were: Alice $+80.15$ over 64 games, Bob $+110.79$ over 86,
persuasion buyer $+34.77$ over 37, persuasion seller $+20.90$ over 41,
negotiation buyer $-20.33$ over 11, and negotiation seller $-13.40$ over nine.

The full bargaining target is the strongest descriptive V33 result: both roles
were positive despite different policies. Persuasion is also positive in both
roles, reversing the seller-side sign observed in several earlier batches.
These signs are consistent with, but do not identify the effects of, Bob's
recovery or seller pacing because no contemporaneous branch ablation was run.
Negotiation is the equally important negative result. Both historically selected
branches lost in V33; role locking does not eliminate opponent, configuration,
time, or rating drift.

All offline family, role, schema, constant-$q$ pacing, and static-bound tests
passed, and there were zero action fallbacks or assignment overshoots. The 500
telemetry entries were HTTP 422 \texttt{reward\_harvest} failures against six
non-UUID component-test IDs: \texttt{v31-negotiation} and
\texttt{v31-persuasion} were queried 84 times each; \texttt{v28-frozen-seller},
\texttt{v29-frozen-alice}, \texttt{v30-frozen-text}, and
\texttt{v31-bargaining} were queried 83 times each. The V33 wrapper overwrote
the V28--V30 synthetic flags, while the V31 IDs never matched the prefix
classifier, so harvesting treated all six as live and retried them after every
assignment. Moreover, the checkpoint computed
\texttt{new\_telemetry} before harvesting, so its zero count did not expose the
new errors to the abort guard. The caught queries could not alter live actions
or ratings, but they reveal classification and monitoring-order bugs and
contaminate the exported telemetry count.

These data do not form a randomized ablation. Family order was fixed, role and
configuration assignment came from live matchmaking, ratings evolved during
the run, and two families were adaptively censored. We therefore report exact
sequential outcomes and role splits without confidence intervals or causal
claims.
