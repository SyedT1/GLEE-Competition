\section{Related work}

GLEE joins economic games with language-agent evaluation
\citep{shapira2024glee}. Automated-negotiation research has long separated
opponent preference, strategy, and deadline modeling \citep{baarslag2016opponent};
learned dialogue agents instead optimize negotiation end to end
\citep{lewis2017deal}. Our explicit separation of numeric policy from fixed
language resembles modular strategy--generation designs
\citep{he2018decoupling}, but we contribute neither a learned dialogue model nor
a new equilibrium solution. Interactive-agent benchmarks emphasize task-level
success and failure \citep{liu2024agentbench}; internal algorithmic audits
emphasize lifecycle evidence \citep{raji2020accountability}, while runtime
shields enforce action-level specifications \citep{alshiekh2018shielding}. Our
narrower contribution aligns strategic output, a contract validator, and an
exposure controller against the same ordered trace; unlike a formal shield, its
validator guarantees action legality rather than reward. The persuasion
inequality below is a direct static Bayes constraint related to Bayesian
persuasion \citep{kamenica2011bayesian}, not a dynamic result. Confidence
sequences address optional stopping \citep{howard2021confidence}, but this trace
does not establish an estimand or the conditional dependence assumptions needed
for a valid anytime analysis.
