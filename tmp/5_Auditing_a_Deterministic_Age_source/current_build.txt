    Abstract

1   We audit ELP-AGENT, a deterministic, role-separated entrant for GLEE bargain-

2   ing, bilateral negotiation, and persuasion. The submitted policy uses no language

3   model at inference time: numeric actions come from explicit opponent-path sum-

4   maries and heuristic thresholds, while validators and a one-assignment controller

5   constrain execution and exposure. Three development failures motivated the au-

6   dit: a legal 99-round cycle, assignment overshoot, and family totals that concealed

7   a losing role. Across the complete 1,704-game dashboard history, displayed

8   changes sum to +535.9/ + 695.0/ + 833.0 for the three families, while sequen-

9   tial role comparisons show observed sign reversals even under unchanged code.

10  In the final adaptively stopped trace, bargaining/persuasion/negotiation changed

11  +190.94/ + 55.67/ - 33.73 over 150/78/20 games. An author-supplied next-day

12  snapshot placed the entrant 95th overall at 1674.3. These outcomes neither es-

13  timate policy effects nor validate the persuasion seller's static Bayes calibration:

14  matchmaking was uncontrolled, policies were selected using earlier live runs, and

15  two families were censored by drawdown guards. The contribution is therefore an

16  executable policy specification and a failure-oriented case study of how strategic

17  decisions, action validity, and exposure monitoring can diverge.

18 1 Scope and contributions

19 GLEE evaluates agents in bargaining, bilateral trade, and persuasion with optional natural-language
20 interaction, heterogeneous horizons, and incomplete information [10]. We study a deliberately in-
21 spectable entrant: its economic actions are produced by fixed Python rules rather than model infer-
22 ence. This makes failures attributable to explicit state summaries, branch logic, validation, or the
23 run controller.

24 This paper makes three bounded contributions. First, it gives a self-contained specification of the six
25 deployed role policies and their memory boundaries (Appendix A). Second, it audits failures that
26 legal-action rate or aggregate rating alone would hide: cycling, uncontrolled exposure, role reversals,
27 and a monitoring-order bug. Third, it releases the exact executable, ordered evidence, analysis code,
28 and a 248-game sequential trace. We do not claim strategic superiority, causal mechanism effects,
29 dynamic receiver obedience, or generalization beyond this run.

30 The methodological novelty is the alignment of three normally separate audit layers--strategic deci-
31 sions, contract enforcement, and sequential exposure-- on one immutable event trace. This decom-
32 position reveals when legal execution, apparently safe monitoring, and rating success disagree; it is
33 an audit design, not a new equilibrium or learning algorithm.

34 2 Related work

35 GLEE joins economic games with language-agent evaluation [10]. Automated-negotiation research
36 has long separated opponent preference, strategy, and deadline modeling [2]; learned dialogue agents
37 instead optimize negotiation end to end [6]. Our explicit separation of numeric policy from fixed
38 language resembles modular strategy-generation designs [3], but we contribute neither a learned
39 dialogue model nor a new equilibrium solution. Interactive-agent benchmarks emphasize task-level
40 success and failure [7]; internal algorithmic audits emphasize lifecycle evidence [8], while runtime
41 shields enforce action-level specifications [1]. Our narrower contribution aligns strategic output, a
42 contract validator, and an exposure controller against the same ordered trace; unlike a formal shield,
43 its validator guarantees action legality rather than reward. The persuasion inequality below is a di-
44 rect static Bayes constraint related to Bayesian persuasion [5], not a dynamic result. Confidence
45 sequences address optional stopping [4], but this trace does not establish an estimand or the condi-
46 tional dependence assumptions needed for a valid anytime analysis.

121 6 Agent behavior audit

122 The executable targets three process properties: individual rationality, escape from repeated paths,
123 and role-separated evidence. Validators test action legality; cycle counters test termination behav-
124 ior; and family/role guards test exposure. None is a certificate of rating performance. Likewise,
125 the seller's deterministic frequency target controls aggregate signaling frequency but not dynamic
126 receiver obedience.

127 The transcript export covers 220 of 248 rated assignments and omits game IDs, so behavior cannot
128 be linked safely to assignment rating. In the 127 available bargaining games, 113 reached agree-
129 ment (47/54 for Alice and 66/73 for Bob). This measures agreement behavior, not the effect of the
130 recovery override.

131 In 40 available seller games, ELP positively signaled all 397 high-quality and 135/403 low-quality
132 products. Of 532 positive signals, 397 were high quality (74.6% available-case precision); opposing
133 buyers purchased 309/532 positive- signal and 24/268 negative-signal products. These aggregate re-
134 sponses neither establish counterfactual purchase lift nor verify obedience. In 34 buyer games, ELP
135 purchased 350/680 products (245 high, 105 low); passed quality is hidden, so this purchased subset
136 is selectively revealed. Negotiation reached 11/19 deals, with four no-deals and four walkaways,
137 while both role totals were negative.

138 The audit's defensible conclusion is diagnostic: action validity, agreement, signal calibration, and
139 rating success are distinct outcomes. The missing ID linkage prevents attributing rating changes to
140 particular behaviors.

141 7 Limitations and conclusion

142 This is one post-selection, fixed-order competition trace. Matchmaking, role, configuration, oppo-
143 nent, rating shrinkage, and time are uncontrolled; two families are adaptively censored. Displayed
144 rating deltas are platform measures, not welfare, and the transcript export is incomplete and unlinked.
145 Fixed text also cannot test whether richer dialogue would help. Strategic persuasion can transfer to
146 manipulative settings; explicit labeling as a calibration heuristic and restriction to the competition
147 reduce but do not remove that concern.

148 The exact executed notebook and evidence are bundled with hashes in artifacts/v33/; the anal-
149 ysis regenerates the statistical appendix tables, derived CSVs, summary, and sequential figure from
150 a clean extraction. Actions present in the append-only evidence can be audited, but exact ratings,
151 missing transcript records, and unrecorded counterfactuals cannot be replayed.

152 Overall, legal execution, behavioral process, exposure, and rating effects must be evaluated sepa-
153 rately; these run totals do not identify mechanism effects.

293 A.4 Contract wrapper and exposure controller

294 The wrapper rejects unexpected keys, nonfinite or negative amounts, allocations that do not sum to
295 the pot within tolerance, illegal decisions, missing nonterminal counteroffers, and messages over
296 2,000 characters. Its fallbacks are: equal split/accept in bargaining; own-value offer, profitable ac-
297 ceptance, or own-value counter in negotiation; PASS/no as seller; and prior expected-value purchase
