Abstract
We audit ELP-AGENT, a deterministic, role-separated entrant for GLEE bargain-
1
ing, bilateral negotiation, and persuasion. The submitted policy uses no language
2
model at inference time: numeric actions come from explicit opponent-path sum-
3
maries and heuristic thresholds, while validators and a one-assignment controller
4
constrain execution and exposure. Three development failures motivated the au-
5
dit: a legal 99-round cycle, assignment overshoot, and family totals that concealed
6
a losing role. Across the complete 1,704-game dashboard history, displayed
7
changes sum to +535.9/ + 695.0/ + 833.0 for the three families, while sequen-
8
tial role comparisons show observed sign reversals even under unchanged code.
9
In the final adaptively stopped trace, bargaining/persuasion/negotiation changed
10
+190.94/ + 55.67/ - 33.73 over 150/78/20 games. An author-supplied next-day
11
snapshot placed the entrant 95th overall at 1674.3. These outcomes neither es-
12
timate policy effects nor validate the persuasion seller's static Bayes calibration:
13
matchmaking was uncontrolled, policies were selected using earlier live runs, and
14
two families were censored by drawdown guards. The contribution is therefore an
15
executable policy specification and a failure-oriented case study of how strategic
16
decisions, action validity, and exposure monitoring can diverge.
17
1 Scope and contributions
18
GLEE evaluates agents in bargaining, bilateral trade, and persuasion with optional natural-language
19
interaction, heterogeneous horizons, and incomplete information [10]. We study a deliberately in-
20
spectable entrant: its economic actions are produced by fixed Python rules rather than model infer-
21
ence. This makes failures attributable to explicit state summaries, branch logic, validation, or the
22
run controller.
23
This paper makes three bounded contributions. First, it gives a self-contained specification of the six
24
deployed role policies and their memory boundaries (Appendix A). Second, it audits failures that
25
legal-action rate or aggregate rating alone would hide: cycling, uncontrolled exposure, role reversals,
26
and a monitoring-order bug. Third, it releases the exact executable, ordered evidence, analysis code,
27
and a 248-game sequential trace. We do not claim strategic superiority, causal mechanism effects,
28
dynamic receiver obedience, or generalization beyond this run.
29
The methodological novelty is the alignment of three normally separate audit layers--strategic deci-
30
sions, contract enforcement, and sequential exposure-- on one immutable event trace. This decom-
31
position reveals when legal execution, apparently safe monitoring, and rating success disagree; it is
32
an audit design, not a new equilibrium or learning algorithm.

2 Related work
34
GLEE joins economic games with language-agent evaluation [10]. Automated-negotiation research
35
has long separated opponent preference, strategy, and deadline modeling [2]; learned dialogue agents
36
instead optimize negotiation end to end [6]. Our explicit separation of numeric policy from fixed
37
language resembles modular strategy-generation designs [3], but we contribute neither a learned
38
dialogue model nor a new equilibrium solution. Interactive-agent benchmarks emphasize task-level
39
success and failure [7]; internal algorithmic audits emphasize lifecycle evidence [8], while runtime
40
shields enforce action-level specifications [1]. Our narrower contribution aligns strategic output, a
41
contract validator, and an exposure controller against the same ordered trace; unlike a formal shield,
42
its validator guarantees action legality rather than reward. The persuasion inequality below is a di-
43
rect static Bayes constraint related to Bayesian persuasion [5], not a dynamic result. Confidence
44
sequences address optional stopping [4], but this trace does not establish an estimand or the condi-
45
tional dependence assumptions needed for a valid anytime analysis.

6 Agent behavior audit
121
The executable targets three process properties: individual rationality, escape from repeated paths,
122
and role-separated evidence. Validators test action legality; cycle counters test termination behav-
123
ior; and family/role guards test exposure. None is a certificate of rating performance. Likewise,
124
the seller's deterministic frequency target controls aggregate signaling frequency but not dynamic
125
receiver obedience.
126
The transcript export covers 220 of 248 rated assignments and omits game IDs, so behavior cannot
127
be linked safely to assignment rating. In the 127 available bargaining games, 113 reached agree-
128
ment (47/54 for Alice and 66/73 for Bob). This measures agreement behavior, not the effect of the
129
recovery override.
130
In 40 available seller games, ELP positively signaled all 397 high-quality and 135/403 low-quality
131
products. Of 532 positive signals, 397 were high quality (74.6% available-case precision); opposing
132
buyers purchased 309/532 positive- signal and 24/268 negative-signal products. These aggregate re-
133
sponses neither establish counterfactual purchase lift nor verify obedience. In 34 buyer games, ELP
134
purchased 350/680 products (245 high, 105 low); passed quality is hidden, so this purchased subset
135
is selectively revealed. Negotiation reached 11/19 deals, with four no-deals and four walkaways,
136
while both role totals were negative.
137
The audit's defensible conclusion is diagnostic: action validity, agreement, signal calibration, and
138
rating success are distinct outcomes. The missing ID linkage prevents attributing rating changes to
139
particular behaviors.
140
7 Limitations and conclusion
141
This is one post-selection, fixed-order competition trace. Matchmaking, role, configuration, oppo-
142
nent, rating shrinkage, and time are uncontrolled; two families are adaptively censored. Displayed
143
rating deltas are platform measures, not welfare, and the transcript export is incomplete and unlinked.
144
Fixed text also cannot test whether richer dialogue would help. Strategic persuasion can transfer to
145
manipulative settings; explicit labeling as a calibration heuristic and restriction to the competition
146
reduce but do not remove that concern.
147
The exact executed notebook and evidence are bundled with hashes in artifacts/v33/; the anal-
148
ysis regenerates the statistical appendix tables, derived CSVs, summary, and sequential figure from
149
a clean extraction. Actions present in the append-only evidence can be audited, but exact ratings,
150
missing transcript records, and unrecorded counterfactuals cannot be replayed.
151
Overall, legal execution, behavioral process, exposure, and rating effects must be evaluated sepa-
152
rately; these run totals do not identify mechanism effects.

Shared notation and state. clip(z, a, b) = max(a, min(b, z)) and (z) = (1 + e-z)-1. For
195
known horizon R at round r, t = (r - 1)/ max(1, R - 1); otherwise t = min(0.65, (r - 1)/16). A
196

197

198

199

200

201

202

203
